We analyze interrupted time-series data from a mobile health intervention study targeting college students' psychological well-being using a hierarchical Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) process model. The OU model's main parameters capture intraindividual variability, an attractor (homeostasis) point, and an elasticity parameter that governs the speed with which the process returns to its attractor after a perturbation.

We explore BayesFlow, a simulation-based inference method that uses neural networks for efficient Bayesian parameter estimation. Implementing BayesFlow in our multilevel hierarchical model demands careful consideration, and we discuss our strategies and potential future extensions.
